# Batch 1 Portrait Prompts

Ready-to-paste generation prompts for the full current roster (33 members), per [STYLE_GUIDE.md](STYLE_GUIDE.md)'s Process step 1. Each entry is self-contained — the baseline register (tone, palette, composition, line quality) is restated in every prompt so nothing depends on external context once pasted into a generation tool.

Save each result as `public/portraits/<id>.png`, portrait-oriented, thumbnail-appropriate resolution (dossier-sidebar use only — see STYLE_GUIDE.md's Downstream use note; not yet verified for 3D-texture use).

**Likeness tier**, per STYLE_GUIDE.md's known-vs-unknown-likeness section:
- **Photographed** — aim for recognizable likeness against surviving photographs.
- **Character study** — no photographic ground truth; period-appropriate study, not likeness reproduction. Where the figure has an existing religious/devotional iconographic tradition, the prompt favors a secular framing over reproducing that iconography.

One flagged exception: **Amadou Bamba** has a single surviving photograph that is treated with particular reverence within the Muridiyya tradition (widely reproduced, near-devotional status). The prompt below aims for general recognizable likeness (dress, bearing, complexion) without directly reproducing that specific photograph's composition — worth your own judgment call on review, not something to generate on autopilot.

## Julian of Norwich (`julian`) — c.1342–after 1416 — Character study — REVISED v4 — DELIBERATE COMPOSITION DEVIATION, see note below

Warm, etching-adjacent portrait of Julian of Norwich, English anchoress, later 14th century. Unlike the standard head-and-shoulders composition used for the rest of this set, frame her as seen *through* a small stone window aperture — the historical "anchorhold squint," the actual physical opening through which an enclosed anchoress spoke to visitors (a specific architectural/biographical fact, not devotional imagery). Her face and shoulders are visible within the window's dark stone frame, partially shadowed but not obscured.

She should read as **elderly — in her 50s or 60s, not young.** She is remembered chiefly for the Long Text, completed some twenty years after her 1373 visions at thirty — the product of two decades of contemplation, not a young woman's account — and that maturity should be visible: a weathered, deeply lined face, silver or grey hair at the edges of the wimple.

Dress: a coarse black or undyed wool habit, plainer and less voluminous than a formal monastic order's habit (she belonged to no order — anchoresses lived under local, diocesan discipline, not a rule like the Benedictines), a simple linen wimple and veil pinned close, consistent with 14th-century English anchoritic dress as described in guides of the period like the *Ancrene Wisse*. This should read as visibly distinct in cut and fabric from Hildegard's 12th-century German Benedictine habit elsewhere in this set — plainer, rougher, two centuries and a different country apart. Downcast or averted eyes, withdrawn expression.

**On anonymity — scope it precisely:** no likeness tradition exists for her *face* specifically, and even her given name is lost to history — that's what should stay unspecific. Everything else about her (age, era, region, the material facts of anchoritic dress and enclosure) is well documented and should be rendered with full period confidence, not vaguely. The goal is a specific, dateable 14th-century Englishwoman glimpsed through a window, not a generic or ahistorical "nun" figure — anonymity of identity is not license for vagueness of detail.

Favor this secular, architectural framing over any devotional staging. Visible linework and texture (engraving/ink-wash register), not photorealistic or cartoon/flat-vector. Limited warm sepia/candlelit palette, consistent across a set. Portrait-oriented, thumbnail resolution.

**Note on the deviation:** STYLE_GUIDE.md's baseline composition is head-and-shoulders against a plain dark background, no scene elements. This prompt breaks that rule deliberately for Julian only — the stone window *is* the scene element, and it's the single most historically specific, load-bearing fact available for a figure who otherwise has zero likeness tradition and a name that isn't even her own. Flagging per STYLE_GUIDE.md's own instruction to record any deviation rather than let it drift silently. Worth Rachel's explicit sign-off before running, since it's a bigger swing than v2 was.
