Behind the veil of all the hieratic and mystical allegories of ancient doctrines, behind the darkness and strange ordeals of all initiations, under the seal of all sacred writings, in the ruins of Nineveh or Thebes, on the crumbling stones of old temples and on the blackened face of the Assyrian or Egyptian sphinx, in the monstrous or marvellous paintings which interpret to the faithful of India the inspired pages of the Vedas, in the cryptic emblems of our old books on alchemy, in the ceremonies practised at reception by all secret societies, there are found indications of a doctrine which is everywhere the same and everywhere carefully concealed.

Magic is the traditional science of the secrets of Nature which has been transmitted to us from the Magi. By means of this science the adept is invested with a species of relative omnipotence and can operate superhumanly — that is, after a manner which transcends the normal possibility of men. The two great secrets of practical magic are: a force to be determined, and a direction to be given to that force.

The force is one. It is the great magical agent, the Astral Light. Its direction depends entirely on the will. The magician who does not know how to will, will accomplish nothing. The magician who knows how to will but not how to wait will spend his force on the air. The whole art of magic consists in knowing how to want a thing without wanting it too soon, without wanting it too long, without wanting it too personally.

I say this knowing that most of my readers will not understand it, and most of those who believe they understand it will be wrong. But the words are here, and the time comes for each reader when the words open.
