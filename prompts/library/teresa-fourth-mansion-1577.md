Before I continue, I want to explain what I mean by consolations, and how these differ from what I have called spiritual sweetnesses. I think there is a real difference between them, though it is not easy to say so in a way that will be understood by those who have not experienced it.

Sweetness in prayer comes to us, as I see it, partly through ourselves — assisted always by God, for without His help we can do nothing, not even form a good thought. But the sweetness arises from the nature of the virtuous work itself, and it seems as if we had merited it by our own labours. That is its characteristic: it begins in ourselves and ends in God.

Consolations are another matter entirely. They begin in God. Our nature feels them, but it does not comprehend them. They expand the heart; they do not narrow it. I have tried many times to find a comparison for this expansion. What comes to me is water. When water springs from its source, it remains still; it does not overflow, yet the basin fills. There is no sound of rushing. This is how God works in the soul when it has reached the Fourth Mansion — not forcefully, not noisily, but with a fullness that leaves no room for fear.

What surprises me in this state is not the peace, but the clarity. Things that seemed obscure present themselves plainly. The soul does not argue; it knows. I do not say it knows everything. I say that what it knows, it knows with a certainty that no argument could give and no argument can take away.
